# Revival

## Overview

In Revival, you manage a construction company cashing in on a city in its second act. A new mayor
has been elected, determined to turn the city around, and is willing to engage the private sector.

The city is run down: every empty plot is a blighted lot. Each turn, your company must choose one:
break ground on a new development, or finish an existing project. The mayor will walk the streets to
inspect your work, leaving tax incentives on empty lots and taxing projects on built-up corners.

The company with the most money at the end of the mayor's term will be declared the winner.

## Setup

1. **City**: lay out the board.
2. **Historical**: shuffle the 8 setup cards and deal one beside each quadrant, upright. Place every
   building on it whose badge is at least the number of players (no badge: always). A historical
   Park is a supply Park, finished side up.
3. **Mayor**: stand the mayor on City Hall.
4. **Agenda**: shuffle the 24 agenda cards and count out the game's deck (table); put the rest away
   unseen.
5. **Row**: shuffle the building tiles and deal one, construction side up, into each of the row's
   first slots (table), from the +0 end. Each slot adds to its tile's cost: **+0, +0, +1, +1, +1,
   +2, +2, +3**. Parks go in a supply beside it.

| Players | Tiles in row | Agenda cards | Historical buildings |
| ------- | ------------ | ------------ | -------------------- |
| **2**   | 5            | 13           | 20                   |
| **3**   | 6            | 13           | 16                   |
| **4**   | 7            | 12           | ~14                  |
| **5**   | 8            | 11           | 12                   |

6. **Companies**: each player takes a company mat (credit rating 0) and 3 workers. The first player
   gets 4 coins, the second 5, and so on.
7. **Opening walk**: reveal the top agenda card. The first player walks the mayor there from City
   Hall: **Target** and **Stops** only (below). Discard it, then reveal 2 face up beside the board:
   the mayor's agenda.

## The city

The city is a grid of **blocks** of 1–4 plots, with streets between them. The **corners** of an
intersection are the 4 plots touching it, one in each block round it; every plot is a corner of
exactly one intersection. City Hall (C3) has no corners: its plots are the plaza.

## Your turn

Do **one** action. You can't pass.

- **Break ground** on an empty plot: put down a tile and one of your workers.
  - **Row tile**: pay its cost (gold coin) plus its slot's surcharge.
  - **Park**: free, from the supply. Discard the tile in the +0 slot.
  - Slide the row's tiles toward +0 to close the gap.
  - Take any incentives on the plot.
- **Finish** one of your projects: take your worker off, flip the tile to its finished side, and
  **sell it** (below). If it's one of the mayor's corners, take 1 incentive.

**End of turn**: if **2 or fewer tiles** are left in the row, you're the **walker**: the mayor
walks. Then refill the row's empty slots from the deck.

## Selling

Values are on your sale card. Only **finished** buildings count; a tile with a worker on it counts
for nothing.

- **Park**: 1.
- **House**: 3, +1 per Park in its block, +? per Industrial it raises (penciled in). **Can't be
  finished until every plot in its block has a tile** (with workers or not).
- **Commercial**: 2, +1 per House it raises.
- **Industrial**: 1, +3 per Commercial it raises.
- **Raising**: a finished building stays flat until another building draws on it; then put a wooden
  building of its type on it (**raise** it). Count the finished buildings of the seller's type in
  its block, itself and historical ones included: **n**. Raise **up to 1 + n** flat buildings it
  draws on (you must raise what you can): its own block first, then
  - **Commercial** (draws on Houses): the blocks across the street (up to 4).
  - **Industrial** (draws on Commercials): the blocks in its row and column.
  - **House** (draws on Industrials): reach penciled in.

## The mayor's walk

1. **Target**: the walker picks one of the 2 face-up agenda cards, and an L-shaped path to it along
   the streets (at most one turn).
2. **Stops**: every intersection the mayor steps onto, target included (not his start). At each, the
   first that applies:
   1. **An empty corner** → the walker puts 1 incentive on one (they stack).
   2. **Otherwise** → **tax**: each company pays 1 coin per worker it has on these corners. Can't
      pay: abandon that many of those plots (your pick); the tile leaves the game, workers come
      back.
3. **Credit**: every company collects its credit rating in coins.
4. **Upgrades**: everyone **except the walker** may spend incentives (mat):
   - Credit rating +1 step for 2 / 3 / 4 incentives (max 3).
   - +1 worker for 3 incentives (max 5).
5. **Agenda**: discard the target and reveal a new card, if any are left.

## End of the game

The game ends after the walk to the **last agenda card**. Then:

1. **Final sale**: every company may finish one project at full value.
2. **Firesale**: every other project with a worker sells for its base value − 1: House 2, Commercial
   1, Industrial 0, Park 0. No Parks or raising.
3. **Most coins wins.**
